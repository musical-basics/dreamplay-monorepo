# Marketing positioning: the 45+ returning player

**Date** 2026-08-14 · **Source** business coach discussion + AB Test August 10 survey (18 responses) + founder call with Nigel Binns

This is a strategic direction, not a finished plan. It records the reasoning
so the next person (or the next context window) can pick it up without
re-deriving it.

---

## 1. The realization

**More than half our buyers are over 45.** From the 18 survey responses, 17
gave an age:

| Age | n | Share of stated |
|---|---|---|
| 18 to 29 | 5 | 29% |
| 30 to 44 | 2 | 12% |
| **45 to 59** | **6** | **35%** |
| **60 or older** | **4** | **24%** |
| Prefer not to say | 1 | - |

**10 of 17 (59%) are 45 or older**, and 45-59 is the single largest bracket.
This is not a youth product, and it is not a family product.

**Nobody is buying for a child.** 17 of 18 bought for themselves; the one
exception was a teacher buying for a student. Zero parent purchases. The
conclusion we are drawing: **the parent market is not obtainable at this
stage**, and should not receive marketing effort.

**They are returners, not beginners.** "Returning after years away" is the
largest experience group (8 of 18), ahead of intermediate (4), professional
or teacher (2), advanced (2), and just starting (2). We are selling to people
who **stopped playing and want back in**.

**The physical problem is nearly universal.** 15 of 18 cannot reach an octave
comfortably: 8 "with some strain", 7 "barely", only 3 "easily".

---

## 2. Why this changes the strategy

We have a **niche product with the potential of the larger piano-playing
market**, but we also have a **1-year delivery timeline**. Those two facts
together mean we cannot afford to market broadly right now. We have to
concentrate on the cohort most likely to convert, and that cohort is 45+.

The logic: a customer willing to pay roughly $1,000 and then wait a year is
not making a casual purchase. They are resolving something. The 45+ returning
player has a specific thing to resolve, and it is not aspiration.

**What this group is not motivated by:**

- Becoming a concert pianist. That ship has sailed and they know it.
- Their kids. They are not buying for children.
- Specifications, dimensions, millimeter measurements.
- Institutional adoption and credibility signals.

**What this group is motivated by:** the feeling that **something they were
previously blocked from is now unblocked**.

That is the core theory. Everything below is downstream of it.

---

## 3. The emotional thesis

> Reaching the right notes and avoiding strain are not the product. They are
> in service of **playing the music they love**.

Our current landing page is sterile. It talks about numbers, dimensions, and
adoption. For a $1,000 purchase with a 1-year wait, none of that is what
moves someone. What moves them is recognizing their own experience in the
page: they slowed down on piano because it stopped being enjoyable, and they
assumed that was just what getting older meant.

Nigel Binns (call, 14 Aug) is the archetype. 64, playing since 15, Dupuytren's
contracture, 7-inch left-hand reach. He owns a Yamaha acoustic upright in
Scotland that he cannot play as written. He practiced for hours and saw
almost no improvement, because the barrier was never effort. It was geometry.
**He did not lose the ability to want to play. He lost the ability to play
what he wants.** That is the customer.

---

## 4. Messaging directions

Raw thoughts from the coaching discussion, kept close to the original wording
because the phrasing is the point:

**The core promises**

- Play the pieces you always wanted to play.
- Play the music you always wanted to play.
- Lifelong passion.
- You don't have to give up the piano as you get older.

**The recognition (name the thing they already feel)**

- There was a point where you slowed down on piano because it wasn't
  enjoyable anymore.
- The piano *is* enjoyable, and you can enjoy it for the rest of your life.
- How can you continue enjoying playing for the rest of your life?

**The question to ask outright**

> **Is the piano still fun?**

Asking this directly is likely stronger than any claim we could make. It
invites the visitor to answer honestly, and for our target cohort the honest
answer is "no, not really, not anymore." That is the opening.

**Socialization angle**

For older players specifically, there is a social dimension worth exploring:
playing with others, community, not practicing alone. Underdeveloped so far,
flagged for thought rather than action.

## 5. Commercial experiments to run

These are separate from messaging and can be tested independently.

**Advertise a smaller deposit. Test $249.**

Grounded in the order book: since April, **14 of 18 buyers (78%) chose the
50% deposit**, at a median $549 deposit implying roughly a $1,098 order.
The established read is that **the price is acceptable, the lump sum is
not**. A $249 entry point attacks exactly that friction. See §7a of the
AB test findings for the full order-book analysis.

**Make the Pro model sold out.**

Scarcity signal. Also has the effect of concentrating demand on the
configurations we most want to build.

---

## 6. The delivery timeline problem (flagged, not decided)

We may need to go back to advertising a **6-month timeline** rather than
1 year, on the theory that a year is simply too long for people to accept.

**This is recorded because it was raised, not because it is decided.** Lionel
noted himself that it is "kind of a lie" and that it is a separate
conversation. It should stay a separate conversation. The AB test findings
already identified that *doubts a new company could deliver* was the most
common hesitation (5 of 18), and that this "resolves when units ship, which
needs capital, not copy."

A shortened date that is then missed would damage exactly the trust that our
most fragile segment is already worried about. If the timeline changes, it
should change because manufacturing changed. Worth a deliberate decision with
its eyes open, not a marketing default.

## 7. What this implies for the site

Concrete next steps, in rough priority order:

1. **Rewrite the hero around the emotional promise**, not specifications.
   Candidate: lead with "Is the piano still fun?" or "Play the pieces you
   always wanted to play."
2. **Demote dimensions and institutional adoption** further down the page.
   Keep them, they matter to a subset, but they are not the opening argument.
3. **Tell the return story explicitly.** These buyers quit. We are how they
   come back. This was already recommendation §9 of the AB test findings;
   the age data now says it should be the *primary* frame, not one of several.
4. **Test the $249 deposit** as a headline offer.
5. **Mark the Pro model sold out.**
6. **Build the sizing guide** (already an open action from the AB test, made
   more urgent by Nigel's call): hand span to DS5.5 / 6.0 / 6.5.

---

## 8. Honest caveats

- **n = 18.** This is a real signal and it is consistent with the founder
  calls, but it is one survey of people who already bought. It tells us who
  our *buyers* are, not who the *market* is.
- **Survivorship bias applies to age as much as to price.** We know 45+
  people converted. We do not know that younger visitors are unreachable,
  only that they are not who converted so far.
- **"The parent market is not obtainable" is a decision about focus, not a
  proven fact.** Zero parent purchases out of 18 is suggestive at this
  sample size, not conclusive. The right framing is that it does not merit
  effort *at this stage*, which is exactly how it is stated above.
- The site currently converts approximately nobody (249 scored sessions,
  63 configurations, 13 begin_checkout, **zero purchases**). That is the
  actual problem this repositioning is trying to solve.
